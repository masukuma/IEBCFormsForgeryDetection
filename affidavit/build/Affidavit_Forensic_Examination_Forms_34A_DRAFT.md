## METHOD

9. **THAT** I examined each annexed page in the steps set out in this and the following six paragraphs. Each step can be repeated with the same files and the software I used, which I produce as annexure [XX]-1. I first identified the original of each annexed page. I compared the page with every candidate original using image features that are unaffected by changes of scale, rotation and camera angle. All Forms 34A share one printed layout, so every candidate matches a page to some degree. The original of a page is distinguished by its handwriting, signatures and stamps, which add several hundred further matching points. I accepted a match only where it was strong and at least twice as strong as the next best candidate.
10. **THAT** I then aligned each annexed page with its original by a geometric transformation that removes differences of size, angle, cropping and perspective, followed by a smooth correction for the curl of the paper. The correction is measured over areas several times the size of a figure and cannot move a single figure, so it cannot conceal an alteration. I compared only the paper of each image and excluded the desk or other background visible around a photographed sheet.
11. **THAT** I then corrected the two images for differences of paper tone, contrast and sharpness, area by area, since a photograph is not equally sharp across the sheet. I compared every mark on the form, that is each digit, letter and stroke, with the same place on the other image, using a correlation measure that is unaffected by brightness and contrast. The measure runs from −1 to 1, and an unchanged mark scores close to 1. I measured every figure containing a doubtful mark again in windows the size of a single digit, so that a changed digit is measured on its own even where it touches the next one. A figure scoring below 0.5 differs from the original. A figure scoring between 0.5 and 0.65 may differ, as when a 3 is made from an 8, and I report it as differing only where it reads differently on the two images. For each figure that differs I prepared an image showing the original and the annexed page side by side.
12. **THAT** photographs of printed copies often lose the grey printed lettering of a form while the handwritten entries remain dark. So as not to mistake faded print for an alteration, I identified the printed parts of each original by overlaying other Forms 34A on it, since the print is common to all of them while the handwritten entries are not. I did not count faded print, marks that are present but fainter, or marks added to a copy for the court bundle, such as captions, page numbers and stamps, as differences in the entries.
13. **THAT** a Form 34A is a single sheet of paper, and every photograph or scan of it is a separate capture with its own framing, lighting and background. Two separate captures of the same sheet share its handwriting and stamps but not those features of the capture. An image made from a capture, whether by printing, re-photographing or editing it, carries them over. For each annexed page I therefore measured the proportion of the original's marks that it reproduces in place, and the correlation between the background visible beyond the edge of the sheet in the original and the same area of the annexed page. Read with the comparison, these measurements distinguish four cases. Where the figures agree and the page carries the original's capture, the page is a copy of the portal image. Where the figures differ and the page carries the original's capture, the page was made from the portal image and its figures were changed. Where the figures differ and the page reproduces the original's handwriting but not its capture, it is another image of the same signed sheet with its figures changed. Where most of the original's marks are absent, the page is not a copy of that form.
14. **THAT** I recorded the SHA-256 hash value of each original and validated its digital signature, which shows whether the file has changed since it was signed.
15. **THAT** the figures I quote in my findings were read from the side-by-side images twice: by me, and independently by a vision language model run through Ollama Cloud. The two readings agree for 99 of the 104 figures I quote. Where they differ, I say so. The finding that a figure differs does not depend on how it is read.

240. **THAT** the pages that differ are 38 of the 39 pages captioned "AGENT COPY" and the page captioned "IEBC (INITIAL) PORTAL" for Kagera Primary School. The one page captioned "AGENT COPY" that agrees with its original is the page for Maragi Primary School. Every other annexed page, including every page captioned "IEBC PORTAL", agrees in its entries with the portal original.
241. **THAT** on every page that differs, the votes of William Ruto are lower than on the original. On 38 of those pages at least one total of valid votes is lower as well. No figure for Raila Odinga differs on any page.
242. **THAT** for the 35 polling stations for which the table in the affidavit of Arnold Ochieng Oginga gives the votes of William Ruto on both forms, its figure for the portal form is the figure on the portal original. Its figure for the agent's form is the figure on the annexed page captioned "AGENT COPY". For Kabete Vetlab Primary School 2 and 3 of 12 and St Martin's School Kibarage 4 of 7, the increase the affidavit gives is the difference between the original and the annexed copy. The variances in that affidavit are therefore differences between each portal original and an annexed copy that differs from it, and not differences between two versions of a form on the portal.
243. **THAT** of the 39 pages that differ, 10 reproduce the original's handwriting together with the background of the portal photograph, and in my opinion were made from that photograph. A further 19 show the same handwritten sheet as the original. For the other 10 my measurements do not settle how the page was made. A photograph of a printed copy often loses the background of the image it was printed from, so a low background correlation does not show that a page was made from a different photograph.
244. **THAT** the comparison shows where an annexed page differs from its original and whether it was made from the same capture. Which version is authentic follows from the custody of the original, being a digitally signed upload to the portal and the physical form, and not from the comparison alone.
245. **THAT** the SHA-1 digest used in the KIEMS signatures is no longer considered secure against deliberate collisions. I therefore treat the signature check as supporting evidence of the integrity of the originals and not as proof on its own.
246. **THAT** photographs of printed copies lose detail. Where an annexed page was too degraded to compare completely, or could not be matched to its original, I have said so.
247. **THAT** I set the thresholds I applied, for accepting a match and for similarity, by testing the method on genuine and altered copies of IEBC forms. The software, its tests and the full numerical results form part of annexure [XX]-1.
248. **THAT** what is deponed to in this affidavit is true to the best of my knowledge, save for matters of opinion, which are my honest opinions.
